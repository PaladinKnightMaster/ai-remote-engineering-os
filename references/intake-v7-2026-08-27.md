**Status:** intake + verification + triage. Seeds the **ADR-0073 product-boundary decision** and the
reference taxonomy that decision produces. Not an artifact regeneration.

**⚠ Doctrine filter — CHANGED, and that is the point.** Intake v4 applied the filter *"runs no

## 2. Delta signals — what this batch actually changes

**1. Independent convergence on ADR-0073's substrate rule — the single most useful finding.**
`maka` (*"does not bundle a shared model connection; users must provide their own"*) and `OpenBot`
(*"no model ships in the box"*) both arrived at **govern the substrate, don't own it** without
reference to us. One is ASF-incubating with 3,949 commits. **This moves the proposed boundary from
"our preference" to "an emerging category norm,"** and ADR-0073 should cite it as such.

**2. The governance seam is now contested, and our claim narrows.** The v6 record held that
`HARNESSROUTER` occupied B-041's seam *"WITHOUT governance."* **`OpenBot` occupies it WITH
governance** — policy gateway, pre-execution decision, audit trail, per-agent isolation — and `maka`
brings an append-only log plus sandbox-boundary approval. **Our differentiator is no longer
"governance." It is governance + air-gap + one signed ledger.** Update the pitch accordingly; this
is the second sobering positioning correction after Microsoft AGT.

**3. Nobody is air-gapped — verified across all sixteen repos.** Not one runs fully offline in
default configuration *with* a governance layer. `magnitude` is offline but ungoverned; `OpenBot` and
`maka` are governed but need cloud or model keys; `HolyClaude`, `headlong`, `OpenExecutive` and
`Chanakya` all require external APIs. **That gap is the product**, and this is now confirmed against
a materially tougher field than the v5/v6 batches.

**4. The star/commit mismatch is a reliable screening heuristic — promote it.** OpenExecutive
1.5k★/**16**, HolyClaude 2.5k★/**51**, rome 374★/**50**, OpenBot 3.2k★/**152**. Same signal already
recorded against openworker (14k★/130 commits). **Stars measure attention; commits measure whether
anyone is still there.**

**5. Licensing blocked or clouded 5 of 17 (~30%).** Two AGPL-3.0 (`proliferate`, `Gotcha`), two with
no license file (`mobile-opencode-control`, `voice-typing-linux`), one unresolved (`Chanakya`).
License verification stays a first-class screening step, not a footnote.

**6. The README-vs-reality trap recurred.** `voice-typing-linux` advertises local voice typing and
ships a network client. Precedent: KittenTTS's false "25MB int8" claim. **Read the architecture, not
the pitch** — now two instances, so it is a pattern.

## 3. Mechanisms to steal (design upgrades, not adoptions)

1. **`munder-difflin`'s "steer → constrain → stop" ladder.** Our approval is **binary** —
   allow/deny. A graduated ladder is strictly richer and speaks directly to residual **R1**
   (`remote.exec` can still do everything the file tools do, less legibly). A "constrain" rung is
   the missing middle between approving a command and refusing it.
2. **The Vercel course's swappable sandbox abstraction** (local / in-memory / remote, one interface).
   This is how "operator-provided execution substrate" becomes implementable **without committing to
   a backend** — directly relevant to B-040 whenever it re-enters.
3. **`maka`'s approval-on-boundary-crossing.** Approval is triggered by *the tool crossing the
   sandbox boundary*, not by a per-tool flag. Ours is per-tool (`requires_approval` on the catalog
   entry). Boundary-derived approval is harder to misconfigure.

- **`mnemosyne`** — embeddings + pgvector. The L3 gate is the embedding/AI dependency, and this is

- **`proliferate`, `Gotcha`** — AGPL-3.0. We redistribute an offline bundle; AGPL governs the

- **`Awesome-Graph-Engineering`** — does not address the problem it was filed against.

## 5. Open question this batch raises for ADR-0073

`OpenBot` and `maka` each govern with a record — CEL policy plus an audit trail, and an append-only
log, respectively. **Neither documents a cryptographic chain, signed heads, or an offline
third-party verifier.**

⚠ **Weigh that as README-level absence, not proven absence** — this pass read documentation, not
source, and "not described" is not "not present." Stated as a positive claim instead: **our
ADR-0069 signed chain plus `aireos-verify` is the only externally-verifiable evidence record
*documented* anywhere in this batch, as of 2026-08-27.**

That is the sharpest form of the "one ledger" thesis — *not that we log, but that an outsider
holding no database access can check the log offline.* **The claim stops there on purpose.**
ADR-0069 §D5 has `audit-service` holding its own signing key, so a compromise of that service is a
stated residual, and any wording implying the record is unforgeable by an insider inflates the claim
past what shipped. ADR-0073 makes
the ledger's **external verifiability** — not merely its existence — the membership test, in exactly
those narrower terms.
